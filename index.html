How it works

Read it early. Move your feet.

What it trains

Picking up the length from the bowler's action and the first part of the flight, and turning it into the right foot movement before the ball pitches.

Why it's built this way

  • Blackout. At higher levels the screen goes black at or just after release, so you have to use early information. In a 2024 review of 12 training studies, this kind of practice clearly improved anticipation, on video tests and on field tests. The studies are small (Müller et al., 2024).
  • See it, then move. Stand in your stance and make the real step as you press. Batters anticipate better when they answer with a movement than with a word (Mann, Abernethy & Farrow, 2010), and in training studies with club and state-squad batters, adding movement to watching carried over better to new bowlers (Brenton, Müller and colleagues, 2019).
  • Real conditions. Real pace, flight times and bounce, club-level accuracy, bowlers taking turns, the real gap between balls. Practice should look like the game (Pinder, Davids, Renshaw & Araújo, 2011).
  • Where to look. Eyes on the hand at release; good batters' eyes then jump ahead to where the ball will pitch (Land & McLeod, 2000, a study of three batsmen).
  • Hard enough, not too hard. The level moves after every ball to keep you right about 85% of the time. Practice works best when it stretches you without swamping you (Guadagnoli & Lee, 2004). The exact 85% comes from maths on simple two-choice learning (Wilson et al., 2019); for batting it is a sensible setting, not a proven best.
  • Feedback fades. Full replays early in each stage, then only after mistakes, so you learn to trust your own read (Salmoni, Schmidt & Walter, 1984).
  • Mixed, not blocked. Lengths, lines and bowlers are mixed. In lab tasks, mixed practice feels harder but lasts and transfers better (Shea & Morgan, 1979).
  • Short and often. About 10 minutes a day beats one long session a week.

How you know it's working

Your score in training stays near 85% on purpose: when you improve, the level rises instead. And how you do during practice is a poor guide to what you've really learned (Soderstrom & Bjork, 2015). So the app has a Skill check: the same 24 balls' worth of test every time, with no feedback, taken fresh about once a week. That number, your level, and above all what happens in your real nets are the things to watch.

What counts as a good read

The right foot (and play or leave), before the ball pitches (+30 ms grace), and after a real look. A press that comes too soon after release to have used the ball's flight counts as a guess: half marks, no XP. XP comes only from good reads, never from speed or volume.

What a screen can't do

  • No real depth. A flat screen has no stereo vision. Life-size view gets the ball's size and timing right, not its depth.
  • It can't see your feet. You're scored on the decision you press, not your footwork. Use the batting mirror to check your head.
  • No bat, no ball, no fear. You don't learn to hit here, and a screen ball can't hurt you. Real nets come first.
  • Simulated bowlers. Real bowlers have their own actions and tells. Here the cues before release are real in kind but deliberately weak and noisy.
  • Carry-over is not guaranteed. Reviews of this kind of training find that gains on screen tests are common, while gains in real matches are smaller and less well proven (Zentgraf, Heppe & Fleddermann, 2017). Treat this as one part of your preparation.
  • Timing. Every press is measured from the frame that showed the release, using the press's own timestamp. The screen and controller still add a few milliseconds; the reaction test uses the same path, so they cancel out of your reading time.

Sync phone + PC

Your sessions, level, career and badges are merged across every device you connect. Nothing is lost: both sides are combined.

Connect (once on each device)

  1. On github.com: Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens → Fine-grained tokens → Generate new token.
  2. Name it “Length Reader”, pick a long expiry, and under Account permissions set Gists to Read and write. Nothing else.
  3. Generate, copy the token and paste it below. Use the same token on your phone and your PC.

Stays per device: settings, calibration, pedals and reaction time (they differ between phone and PC).
Your data is saved as a secret gist in your own GitHub account. Secret gists are unlisted, not encrypted: anyone with the gist's link can read it, so don't share that link. The token stays on this device. There are no accounts of ours and no tracking. It syncs when the app opens and after every session.
